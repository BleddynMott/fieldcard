## Rules (the spec, from G-00c and G-00d, with my rulings where they were silent)

R-x marks a ruling of mine (K03; reversible). Points are each rule's own unit for now. How they add up into Hands and Hunch on the scorecard is for the screen task, after his look pick.

**The field** (G-00c C1)
- The field is the last `size` (500) real finishers of an event, oldest dropped first. Until `size` real players exist, the gap is filled from the newest modelled players, each flagged `modelled`, so the oldest are dropped first (K05 finding 9).
- **Carry-over:** a modelled player is today's truth plus one of yesterday's players' signed errors.
- **R-1.** Hide and Seek have no errors to carry over, so their modelled players are drawn from habit weights by spot kind (in config).

**Dare**
- **Stop's price (V1).** A stop outside the notch pays 0. A stop inside pays 1 to 5, by how thin the field's stops are near it.
  - **R-2, the window:** the field's stops inside the notch are counted within ± `window` of yours (a share of the notch width, clipped at the notch's edges). They're compared with what an even spread across the notch would put in that window.
  - **The price** is linear between 1 (the ratio at or above `crowdedRatio`) and 5 (at or below `rareRatio`), rounded to a whole number.
  - An empty notch (no field stops inside) pays 5.
- **Repeat's bank (V1).** Each round's price is how many in the field cleared it, as 1 ÷ share, rounded to 0.1, and clamped to 1 to `maxPrice` (a share of 0 pays `maxPrice`).
  - Banking pays the sum of the prices of the rounds cleared.
  - Failing a round you chose to try pays 0.
  - Clearing the last round banks it.
- **Long jump (V3).** A take-off over the line is a no-jump and scores 0. Otherwise the score is the share of the field you beat, 0 to 100. Further back is worse, a field no-jump is beaten, and ties count half.
- **Double or bank (V2, as fixed in C7 round 2).**
  - Bank: the base if you were right, 0 if wrong.
  - Double: 2 × the base if right, − the base if wrong.
  - It's offered only on events whose outcome you can't see: Count, Pair, Sort, Hide and Seek.
- **Dare's Stop (R-14 and R-15, his reply of 28 Nov).** `rules.dareStop.mode` picks one:
  - **`notch` (R-14):** Stop's own goes, with a notch 12% of the track wide every day of the week. Plain Stop's notch is 7% narrowing to 2.8%. Only the width changes, so the goes match plain Stop's. It's priced as above.
  - **`open` (R-15, his idea):** no notch. A stop anywhere on the track pays 1 to 5 by how few of the field stopped within ±`openWindow` (3% of the track) of it, on the same go. It's priced as above, with the notch the whole track.
    - The field is hidden while you play. The reveal lays it over the track, with your stop on it.
    - **R-15b:** each go is priced against that go's field, and the event's points are the three go prices added up. In notch mode it stays the middle stop (R-9).
    - **Overlap:** this is Hide's psychology on a moving line ("go where nobody else does"). On a card that also has Hide, the two may feel alike.
- **The rope (V4), R-13 (his "rethink rope", 28 Nov), the default (`snapRule: "load"`):**
  - **The rope holds fewer the higher it climbs:** at ×m it holds `capacity` ÷ m of the field (capacity 1.5: all of it to ×1.5, 3 in 4 at ×2, half at ×3).
  - **It snaps** at the first rung where more of the field is still on than it holds there. "Still on" means a record at that rung or above.
  - **The ceiling:** it never holds past one rung above where all but the bravest tenth (`ceilingShare`) had let go. Without it, a crowd that lets go early leaves no rung overloaded, the rope never snaps, and anyone holding to the top banks ×10 (probe A2, "ceil 0").
  - **If no rung snaps** below the ceiling and the top, the snap is one step past the top, so letting go at ×10 banks it.
  - **Why it doesn't slide:** a crowd that lets go early lifts the snap, so undercutting the crowd is no longer safe. Every quantile rule (the signed median, median plus a rung) settles on one best release or slides (probes A2 and A3).
  - **The reveal line, for the screen task:** "At ×3 the rope holds half. 6 in 10 were still on, so it snapped at ×3. You let go at ×2.5: braver than 6 in 10."
  - **A cost:** it's two rules (the load and the ceiling), where the signed rope had one.
- **The rope, the signed rule (`snapRule: "median"`), kept for comparison:**
  - **The snap** is the multiple by which half of the field had let go: the lower median of the field's release points.
  - **R-8 (after K05 finding 1).** A field player who was snapped counts one `step` *above* the snap, since they were still holding (`snapped: "above"`). It's capped at `max` (round 2, finding 1).
    - The signed wording, "counts at their snap" (`"at"`), makes every record at most the snap, so the snap can only fall: a one-way ratchet, proved in `tests/dare.test.ts`.
    - **What R-8 doesn't fix:** players who undercut by one rung still pull it down (probe A).
    - **What it adds:** the snap can swing between two rungs, so the same release can bank or snap depending on when you play. The field is hidden while you hold, so this reads as chance, not as a bug.
  - **Letting go below the snap** banks the day's points × your multiple, rounded.
  - **Letting go at or above it,** or holding on, keeps `snapKeep` (half) of the day's points.
  - **The ladder** climbs from 1 in steps of `step` (0.5, as signed: "×1, ×1.5, ×2 and on"), up to `max`, never past it.
  - **R-3.** An empty field snaps at `emptySnap`.
- **Hide and Seek (V3).** Each day's playground has `spots` hiding spots, placed by seed and kept apart by `minGap`.
  - Hide scores by the share of the field's seeks that would *not* have found you: a seek finds every spot within `seekRadius`.
  - Seek scores by the share of the field's hiders within `seekRadius` of your tap.
  - Both run 0 to 100.
  - In Dare they're points. In Read they're Hunch.

**Read**
- **Answer blind, then meet the crowd (Q1 and Q2; shared into Dare's Count and Pair).**
  - Hands is your blind answer.
  - If your blind answer and the crowd's answer disagree, you keep or switch.
  - **R-7 (after K05 finding 2 and probe B).** **Keeping** scores +2 if your answer was the closer, −1 if not. **Switching** scores 0. Equally close scores 0.
    - G-00d is split on this. Read 2, Read 4 and ruling 2 say "going with the crowd is safe and earns no Hunch"; Read 3 says both score +1 or −1.
    - Probe B: under both readings, keeping almost never pays: symmetric (switch wins) and keep ±1 with switch 0 (keep loses nearly everywhere). Keep +2 comes from neither reading; it's my choice. It's the only setting tested where keeping pays on some days.
    - Over a year, sharp players do best always keeping and the rest always switching (round 2, finding 6). It's a read of the day only if players can tell trap days, which isn't tested.
    - All four values are in config. His call.
  - If they agree, Hunch is 0.
  - **Count's crowd answer** is the hill's peak: the most common answer. A tie goes to the one closest to the median, and then to the lower.
  - **Pair's crowd answer** is the most common pick among four field players drawn at random. A tie goes to the earliest drawn.
  - **R-11:** each player's four are seeded by card and the player's own key (`fourFor`), so players meet different fours.
- **The Asch line (Q2).** From the field's records: the share right blind, the share right after the four, and the share who switched.
- **Draw (Q3).** A stroke becomes bin heights: the highest point of the stroke over each bin, with gaps filled in a straight line. Those are normalised to shares that sum to 100, and fewer than 2 points means no drawing.
  - **The score** is the overlap with the field's real pile, 0 to 100 (`overlap` in `reads.ts`, as Pour uses).
  - **The badge** is earned when your overlap beats the overlap of everyone's average drawing.
- **Select (Q3, two piles).** One fake beside the real pile, made by a seeded recipe: tidy, spread, shifted, or skewed the other way ("leaning the wrong way"). It's then roughened by seeded noise, so smoothness isn't a tell (K05 finding 6).
  - A shift drops what falls off the end rather than piling it into the end bin.
  - **R-4.** A fake that overlaps the real pile by more than `maxOverlap` is replaced by the next recipe in config order.
  - **R-4b.** If none differs enough, wider shifts are tried. If still none (a pile too flat to fake), it throws, and that day shows another form.
  - Which side the real pile sits on is seeded.
- **Adjust (Q3).** The pile is shown shifted by `s` bins, where s is seeded, at least 1 and at most `maxShift`, never 0. You slide it by `d`. The score is 100 × (1 − |s + d| ÷ `maxShift`), clamped to 0 to 100.
- **Find yourself (Q4).** You guess 0 to 100; the truth is your Hands share beaten. The gap is guess minus truth, and Hunch is −|gap|. The reveal's numbers:
  - the share of the field who guessed above 50;
  - your guess and your truth.

**Other rulings the review found unmarked** (K05 finding 18), now marked **R-10**:
- Repeat's price is 1 ÷ share, capped at `maxPrice` 10. Long jump's ties count half.
- Adjust: doing nothing on a ±1 shift scores 67.
- Count's peak tie goes to the nearest the lower median.
- An empty field scores 50, as `shareBeaten` does; a reveal with no field says so instead (`aschLine` and `thoughtAboveMiddle` return null).
- A stroke is zero outside its span.
- `mostPickedWrong` breaks ties by string order.
- **R-9.** In the simulated Stop field, each player gives one stop, the median of their three (G-00d Q3's "middle stop"). The field counts players, not goes (K05 finding 3).

**Layers**
- **Traps (Z1).** Each event, each card, is a trap with chance `share`, at a strength drawn from `strengthFrom` to `strengthTo`. A trap never falls on the same event two cards running.
  - **R-5, the notch rule (revised, K05 round 1 finding 22 and round 2 finding 4):** the track is split into `thirds` parts. The first go's notch goes in the part where yesterday's field was most often early, at a seeded place inside it.
    - A tie goes to the tied part nearest the middle, then the lower. No early stops goes to the middle.
    - Only the first go: an end part is too narrow to hold three notches `notchApartMin` apart. The other goes are drawn as G-02 draws them, kept apart from it, in the generator task.
  - **The decoy:** yesterday's most-picked wrong answer is returned for the Pair generator to copy. A tie goes to the lowest key.
- **Personality (Z4).** Dare: Bold if your rate of bold choices is above the field's median, otherwise Careful. Read: Loner if your break rate is above the field's median, otherwise Joiner.
  - **R-12.** Dare's bold choices are every dare the card had: V1 (the bare stretch, or going on), V2 (doubling) and V4 (holding past halfway). The caller sums them.
  - No word shows under `minChances` (3, so a single card can earn one), or with no field.
  - The line's numbers (yours and the field's median, counts out of the same chances) are returned with the word.
- **Crowd twin (Z2).** The real field player whose choice vector is nearest to yours (Euclidean). Modelled players are never a twin (K05 finding 8). A tie goes to the earliest in the field's order. A town is returned only if that player opted in.
- **Hide and Seek's kinds:** 12 spots a day from a pool of 20 kinds, so no spot, the shed included, is there every day (K05 finding 5).
