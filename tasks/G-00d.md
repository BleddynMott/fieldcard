## Read 2. The thread, and the slight distinction

The first draft of this file made Read distinct by banning every stake and every crowd shown during play. What was left was round 1's echo, rebuilt from the reads round 1 had cut (review round 1, finding 1). He asked for a *slight* distinction. So both branches keep the crowd on the board, and they differ in the question it poses:

- **Dare:** the crowd sets the **odds**. *How bold dare I be?* You choose safe or bold, and points ride on it.
- **Read:** the crowd gives an **answer**, and it may be wrong. *Is the crowd right?* You go with it or against it. You also draw it, and place yourself in it.

**Read's thread: the crowd's answer is in front of you; read whether to trust it.** This is D1's "trust your eye or the pile", taken as a read and not as a price. The stake is the read itself, chosen by you. **Hunch only moves when you break from the crowd:** up if you were right to, down if you weren't. Going with the crowd is safe and earns no Hunch. That is a choice with something on it (B3's stakes), and the crowd changes what you should do (B3's strategy).

**The difference from Dare is what's judged, not the mechanism.** Breaking from the crowd is a safe-or-bold choice too. In Dare you judge your own nerve, and your points ride on it. In Read you judge the crowd's answer, and only Hunch rides on it.

## Read 3. The reads

Ids are Q1 to Q6. Each is scored the moment you finish, against the field that played before you (R1). The field follows G-00c's rules: the last 500 to finish, plus modelled players at dawn.

**In play: the crowd's answer on your board**
**One rule for both, after the second review: answer blind, then meet the crowd.** You answer first without seeing the crowd. That blind answer is your Hands score, so Hands stays pure perception (test 8). Then the crowd's answer appears, and you **keep** yours or **switch** to theirs. **Hunch moves only when you and the crowd disagree:**
- +1 if your final answer is the closer of the two;
- −1 if it isn't;
- 0 if they're equally close.

Going with the crowd when the crowd is right also scores +1, so trusting a good crowd is a good read. The blind answers are the "before" for the Asch line, with no split crowd needed.

- **Q1. Count: trust the pile.** Count as now, blind. Then the field's answers appear as a small hill over the dial: keep yours, or switch to the hill's peak. Reveal: "7 in 10 switched to the pile. The pile was 2 too low."
- **Q2. Pair: hold or cave** (The Four, B4). Find the pair, blind. Then four earlier players' picks appear as rings: keep yours, or switch to the shape most of the four picked.
  - The four are drawn **at random** from the field; trap days make them fooled on their own, and nothing is hand-picked.
  - Reveal: "Blind, 8 in 10 were right. After the four, 5 in 10." That's the Asch line, the "social expeirment kind of vibe", from today's own blind answers.
  - At dawn the four may be modelled, and are labelled as such.

**Why switching isn't always right.** The crowd's average is usually near the truth, so on a plain day switching wins. On a trap day the crowd is fooled, and keeping a blind answer that saw through it wins. Traps come on unannounced days, in varied kinds and strengths, so that "switch unless it's a trap day" can't be learned. In Read, Z1's reveal line ("This decoy fooled 6 in 10 yesterday") names a trap only after play, and a different kind each time.

**The featured read: one a day, on one event, in his four forms, rotating**
- **Q3. The day's distribution form,** on the axis of the day's featured event: Stop's track, Count's number line, or how far Repeat got. On Stop, the pile uses each player's middle stop of their three. Three forms rotate. His fourth, placing yourself, is Q4, which runs daily.
  - **Draw (the Extraplot day).** One stroke: drag your finger across the axis, the richest form. The reveal builds in order, on the same axis:
    1. the truth;
    2. the field's real pile, as bars;
    3. **everyone's drawings**, as one faint line;
    4. yours, dashed.

    Your own result stays as the small yellow dot on the axis, so you see where you stand. The clutter fix is the order. Each layer arrives alone and the headline names one thing. Nothing is removed. Score: how much of your shape overlaps the real one (round 4's rule).
  - **Select, made simpler, as he asked.** Two piles, both drawn as bars: the real one and one fake. Tap the real one. The fake's recipe varies (too tidy, too spread, leaning the wrong way), and some days the real pile is the tidy one. Reveal: "Most picked the tidy one. The real crowd leaned low. Only 22% spotted it." Score: right or wrong.
  - **Adjust.** You get a pile that is off. Slide it left or right to where the crowd really was. One handle. Reveal: the real pile, everyone's slide as a faint line, and yours. Score: how close.

  **The headline line, every form:** what the crowd did, plus one line of why, from the richest form. For example: "61% stopped late. People wait to see the marker arrive, and by then it's gone." **Beat the crowd:** if your read beats everyone's drawings put together (the faint line), you get the badge "You read the crowd better than it read itself". The crowd's average is usually good, so the badge is rare. That makes it the prize worth sharing, not the everyday score.

**The ending: where people really finished vs where they thought**
- **Q4. Find yourself.** The whole field lines up worst to best on a rank line, by **Hands only**, with no scores shown. Tap where you finished. The piles you've seen give you a feel for the field, not its ranks, so it's a read, not a lookup.
  - **Reveal:** where people really finished, and where they thought they did. "71% thought they beat most of the field. Only half the field can be above the middle. You thought 70%; you beat 57%. 13 points too proud." The teaching line is restored.
  - **Score:** Hunch loses the gap: −13 here.
  - It is his liked "3", restored daily in Read.
  - **The flag, with its beads,** is the Sunday form of this, in both branches (his pick of my recommendation, G-00c Rulings).

**Shared with Dare**
- **Q5. Hide and Seek, unpriced.**
  - Hide scores by how few of the field's seekers would have found you. Seek scores by how many of the field's hiders you'd find.
  - The rules are the same as Dare's V3: Hide first, reveals sealed until both are played, a new playground daily.
  - Both are Hunch.
- **Q6. The heat, once a week.** Watch four real runs, play, then pick the winner. Right or wrong; Hunch.
