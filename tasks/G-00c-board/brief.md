# Brief: brainstorm whole-day concepts for Fieldcard

You are one of four brainstormers. Each one has a different angle and none sees the others. Work from this brief only. Do not read or edit files. This is thinking work. Return at most 1000 words.

## What we are making
A once-a-day phone game, like Wordle or Perceptle, meant to spread. The designer's founding words (typos kept): "like perceptle but what if it was a guess/analysis of crowd behaviour, me vs the world and how to gauge it" and "something that everyone will see go, wow interesting let me try and can fly to top of rankings". The mix he wants: "crowd pyschology, self analysis, meta kind of game mixing graph one with perceptle etc a new novel thing". The graph one is Extraplot: you draw a curve, and the reveal shows "everyones guesses and can see yours overlaid as well as the actual". His original brief: "everyone plays 5 games, and has to guess afterwards how well everyone else did on those games. with a ranking for playing as well as guessing. the key to all of this is marketability, fun, and retention."

Today the card has five short perception events, as in Perceptle: Stop (stop a sweeping marker on a notch), Count (how many figures in a flash wore today's colour), Repeat (tap a flashing sequence back), Pair (find the identical pair of 12 shapes), and Sort (five blobs in size order). These can change. He said of Stop: "game actually is just really easy so makes it less fun". There are two scores: Hands (how you played) and Hunch (how well you read everyone). The look is a school sports day.

## What he has confirmed as interesting (his words)
- Drawing the crowd's distribution: "a good idea, but needs work in terms of how to make interesting, usable, fun and related"; later "Draw it is now slightly cluttered".
- Distribution variants, day to day: "placing yourself on a distribution, drawing the distribution, getting a distribution and adjusting it to match real, selecting correct distribution might all be fun variants day to day".
- "where people really finished vs where they thought" (after play, plant a flag for where you think you finished; reveal everyone's flags against their real finishes).
- Risk: "there should be a good algorithm for going higher and getting it right is high risk high reward, for your overall team perhaps but also for your personal record".
- A weekly "personality" for how you behave: "becoming more of a social experiment kind of vibe". "The week read is cool."
- Teams: "joining a 'team' by university or location ... location vs location battles". A house cup "but needs work: eg is not just random 50/50". A tug-of-war rope: "decent".
- Spot the real crowd (pick the real result among fakes): "good, to make it a bit simpler".
- Watching four other players' runs first, then ranking: "cool as an option once, but doesn't make too much sense to do every time".
- A general structure with different reads mixed and matched across days.

## What he rejected, and why
- Calls about your own performance ("I'm sitting here thinking how well I'm going to do"). A par you call over or under ("no one will want to say I won't beat par").
- Anything with nothing to go on ("how would I possibly be able to guess who is doing better before seeing it").
- Anything physically nonsensical (tap when another player "commits" on a board, since the tap is the commit).
- Anything that needs sitting and thinking, or reveals with layers of extra things to decode. Screens full of sentences and numbers.
- The latest version, verbatim: "don't make it boring though. Now is just you do a task and then just repeat but like if i was everyone what would i pick". That version had five events, each followed by one tap guessing where the crowd went wrong on the same board. It was judged boring: the crowd was an echo of the task, with no tension, nothing at stake, no real people and no surprise.
- Earlier versions: "flat and boring", "low stakes rather than really interesting", "the methods for crowd reading games aren't too interesting".

## Plain-sense rules for any idea
The gesture can happen as asked. The player has something to go on at that moment. It's answerable by gut in a few seconds. The reveal reads at a glance on one picture. And something is at stake or surprising.
